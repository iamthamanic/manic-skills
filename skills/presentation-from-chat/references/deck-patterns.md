# Deck Patterns

Use these as starting points, not rigid templates. Choose the sequence that best supports the desired audience action.

## 1. Sales / Partnership / Affiliate Deck

Best for convincing another company to adopt, integrate, refer, resell, or partner.

Typical narrative:
1. Audience-relevant opportunity or pain
2. Why it matters now
3. Proposed partnership / solution
4. Audience benefits
5. Customer / user benefit
6. How the workflow works
7. Economics or value exchange
8. Proof, capability, and differentiation
9. Implementation effort and risk reduction
10. Concrete next step

Key test: the deck must answer `Why should we do this?`, `Why with you?`, and `How much work is this for us?`.

For cold outbound partner decks, tighten the sequence:
1. One-sentence commercial promise
2. Audience-relevant trigger moment
3. Partner benefit / economics
4. User benefit / timing
5. Integration mechanism
6. Measurement and payout
7. Proof and risk reduction
8. Small pilot
9. Concrete ask

The first three slides must stand alone because many readers skim before deciding whether to continue.

## 2. Internal Decision Deck

Best for leadership decisions, prioritization, budget, architecture, or go/no-go choices.

Typical narrative:
1. Decision required
2. Context and problem
3. Decision criteria
4. Options
5. Evidence and trade-offs
6. Recommendation
7. Expected impact
8. Risks and mitigations
9. Implementation / next steps

Key test: a decision-maker should understand the recommendation without hearing the full meeting history.

## 3. Product / Solution Proposal Deck

Best for proposing a new product, feature, workflow, service, or digital solution.

Typical narrative:
1. User / business problem
2. Who experiences it and when
3. Current alternatives or failure mode
4. Proposed solution
5. User journey or system flow
6. Core capabilities
7. Business value
8. Scope and boundaries
9. Delivery approach / roadmap
10. Decision or ask

Key test: avoid turning the deck into a PRD. Show only the product detail needed to understand and approve the proposal.

## 4. Research / Insight Deck

Best for presenting findings, market analysis, user research, audits, or strategic analysis.

Typical narrative:
1. Question investigated
2. Executive finding
3. Evidence set
4. Major insight 1
5. Major insight 2
6. Major insight 3
7. Implications
8. Recommended actions
9. Limitations / open questions

Key test: separate evidence from interpretation.

## 5. Project / Performance Update

Best for recurring business reviews, project updates, campaign reviews, or stakeholder reporting.

Typical narrative:
1. Executive status
2. Goals / baseline
3. Progress or KPI summary
4. What changed
5. What worked
6. What did not work
7. Risks / blockers
8. Decisions needed
9. Next period priorities

Key test: emphasize deltas, implications, and actions rather than narrating activity.

## 6. Educational / Explainer Deck

Best for teaching a concept, process, regulation, or technical topic.

Typical narrative:
1. Core concept
2. Why it matters
3. Mental model / definition
4. Mechanism or process
5. Examples
6. Common mistakes
7. Practical application
8. Summary / checklist

Key test: optimize for comprehension rather than persuasion.

## Hybrid decks

Use a hybrid only when the objective genuinely crosses categories. Name the primary archetype and borrow individual slide roles from another pattern rather than combining two full templates.
