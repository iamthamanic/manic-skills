# Conversion Deck Principles

Use this when the deck will be sent cold or semi-cold and must persuade without a live presenter.

## Core job

A cold outbound deck is a conversion medium. It must create enough clarity, trust, and motivation for the audience to reply, book a call, or approve a pilot.

## Psychological spine

Use these models ethically:

- Jobs to Be Done: show the job the audience is already trying to accomplish.
- BJ Fogg Behavior Model: motivation, ability, and prompt must all be present.
- EAST: make the desired action easy, attractive, social/proven, and timely.
- Loss aversion: show what the audience misses by doing nothing, without exaggeration.
- Authority and social proof: use certifications, history, customer counts, ratings, and operational proof.
- Friction reduction: answer `how much work is this for us?` early.
- Risk reversal: propose a small pilot before asking for a large integration.

## Cold-deck structure

The first three slides decide whether the reader continues. They must answer:

1. Why should we care?
2. What do we gain?
3. Why is this low effort / low risk?

Recommended flow for partner / affiliate decks:

1. Outcome promise in one sentence.
2. Trigger moment in the partner's user journey.
3. Commercial benefit for the partner.
4. User benefit and timing.
5. Integration mechanism.
6. Measurement / dashboard / payout logic.
7. Why this partner can be trusted.
8. Pilot proposal.
9. Concrete ask.

## Copy rules

- Lead with the partner's gain, not the product category.
- Say the mechanism before the implementation details.
- Use concrete nouns: partner link, order, dashboard, monthly payout, pilot touchpoint.
- Avoid long explanations of the service unless they reduce perceived risk.
- Put proof before asking for commitment.
- Make the next step a single decision, not a menu of options.

## Visual rules

- Show the business model in one diagram.
- Show the user journey in one diagram.
- Show tracking as a dashboard mockup.
- Show proof as a KPI row, not as a dense company biography.
- Move detailed company history, legal details, and secondary services to appendix.
